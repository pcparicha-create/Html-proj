Fun facts about Cricket

  1. The longest cricket match ever basically turned into a small lifetime
    In 1939, South Africa and England played a Test in Durban that lasted timelessly—as in literally no time limit. It went on for 12 days, included nine days of actual play, one day of rain, and two rest days for everyone to remember what the sun looked like. It still didn’t finish. England needed 41 more runs to win, but their boat home was leaving (no Uber back then), so they had to stop the match, pack their bags, and sail away. Nothing says “intense rivalry” like: “Good game, but we gotta catch our ship.”




  2. The ball has a secret “birthday party” each innings
    A cricket ball doesn’t stay the same for long—it goes through life phases faster than a teenager. First 5 overs: bright, shiny, and full of hope. By 20 overs: a bit scuffed, starting to question life choices. By 50 overs: looks like it’s been through a blender. In fact, teams will obsess over the shine like it’s a priceless diamond—rub it, buff it, guard it from anyone who breathes too hard. Swing bowling basically depends on keeping one side glowing and the other looking like it did 50 push-ups on gravel. A cricket ball’s skincare routine is more intense than a YouTuber’s.




  3. One player once scored 365 in a day… and then lived his best life
    Sir Garfield Sobers scored an unbeaten 365 in Test cricket at age 21—young, unstoppable, and possibly powered by pure confidence. That record stood for 36 years. But here’s the fun part: Sobers wasn’t just a batting monster—he bowled pace, spin, left-arm orthodox, chinaman, and probably would’ve bowled right-arm if someone dared him. If cricket had RPG classes, he unlocked the whole skill tree. People joked he was basically five players in one, which made team meetings awkward: “Sobers, you’re opening the batting.” “Also bowling.” “And fielding in slips.” “And finishing the snacks.”
